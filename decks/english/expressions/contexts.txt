# New expressions by context. Format: expression | meaning | example ({{ }} = the expression) | notes | register | context | R
# ---------- IELTS Writing Task 2 (academic essay) ----------
It is widely argued that... | introduces a common opinion you are about to discuss | {{It is widely argued that}} social media has weakened face-to-face communication. | Better than "Nowadays many people think". Avoid "In this day and age", which examiners see as a memorised cliché. | academic | ielts-writing |
a case in point | a clear example of what has just been said | Many traditional industries have been automated; car manufacturing is {{a case in point}}. | Use it right after a general claim. | academic | ielts-writing |
This is not to say that... | prevents the reader from taking your point too far; adds nuance | {{This is not to say that}} technology is harmful, but its effects deserve scrutiny. | Hedging like this is a mark of Band 8+ writing. | academic | ielts-writing |
on balance | after considering all the arguments | {{On balance}}, the advantages of remote work outweigh the drawbacks. | Ideal for the conclusion of a discuss-both-views essay. | academic | ielts-writing |
to a large extent | mostly, though not completely | I agree {{to a large extent}} that governments should fund public transport. | Answers "To what extent do you agree?" precisely. Also "to some extent", "to a limited extent". | academic | ielts-writing |
give rise to | to cause something to happen or exist | Rapid urbanisation has {{given rise to}} severe housing shortages. | More precise than "cause" or "lead to"; usually with negative results. | academic | ielts-writing |
pose a threat to | to be a danger to | Plastic waste {{poses a serious threat to}} marine ecosystems. | Strong collocations: "pose a threat/risk/challenge". | academic | ielts-writing |
exacerbate | to make a problem or bad situation worse | Cutting public funding would only {{exacerbate}} inequality. | Formal synonym of "make worse"; use with problems, not people. | academic | ielts-writing | R
mitigate | to make something bad less severe | Planting trees in cities can {{mitigate}} the effects of heatwaves. | Collocates with: effects, risks, impact. Don't confuse with "militate against". | academic | ielts-writing | R
a double-edged sword | something with both advantages and disadvantages | Globalisation is {{a double-edged sword}} for developing economies. | One of the few idioms acceptable in academic writing. Use once per essay at most. | academic | ielts-writing |
It stands to reason that... | it is logical or obvious that | {{It stands to reason that}} better-paid teachers are more likely to stay in the profession. | | academic | ielts-writing |
notwithstanding | despite | {{Notwithstanding}} these concerns, the policy has been broadly successful. | Formal. Can also follow the noun: "these concerns notwithstanding". | academic | ielts-writing | R
be attributable to | to be caused by | The decline in crime is largely {{attributable to}} better street lighting. | | academic | ielts-writing |
in light of | considering; because of | {{In light of}} recent research, the old guidelines should be revised. | Also "in the light of" (British). | academic | ielts-writing |
there is a growing consensus that | more and more experts agree that | {{There is a growing consensus that}} early childhood education pays long-term dividends. | | academic | ielts-writing |
far-reaching consequences | effects that are wide and long-lasting | Climate policy decisions made today will have {{far-reaching consequences}}. | Also "far-reaching implications". | academic | ielts-writing |
address the issue | to deal with a problem | Governments must {{address the issue}} of youth unemployment urgently. | Avoid "solve the problem" repeatedly; vary with "tackle", "combat". | academic | ielts-writing |
strike a balance between | to find a fair compromise between two things | Cities must {{strike a balance between}} economic growth and environmental protection. | | academic | ielts-writing |
at the expense of | with damage or loss to something else | Many companies pursue profit {{at the expense of}} employee wellbeing. | | academic | ielts-writing |
by contrast | showing a clear difference from the previous point | Urban areas have grown rapidly. Rural regions, {{by contrast}}, have declined. | Use to compare two subjects; "on the contrary" is only for rejecting a statement just made. | academic | ielts-writing |
Admittedly, ... | introduces a point against your own argument (a concession) | {{Admittedly}}, online learning lacks the social side of school. | Follow it with "However, ..." to rebut. Concession + rebuttal = mature argument. | academic | ielts-writing |
it could be argued that | introduces a possible view without fully committing to it | {{It could be argued that}} tourism destroys local culture. | Useful for presenting the opposing view. | academic | ielts-writing |
a contributing factor | one of several causes | Poor diet is {{a contributing factor}} to the rise in heart disease. | More precise than "the reason" when there are several causes. | academic | ielts-writing |
detrimental to | harmful to | Excessive screen time is {{detrimental to}} children's sleep. | | academic | ielts-writing | R
conducive to | creating good conditions for something | A quiet environment is {{conducive to}} effective study. | Opposite of "detrimental to". | academic | ielts-writing | R
play a pivotal role in | to be extremely important in | Parents {{play a pivotal role in}} shaping their children's values. | Alternatives: "a crucial/key/vital role". Don't overuse "play an important role". | academic | ielts-writing |
the root cause | the fundamental reason behind a problem | Poverty is often {{the root cause}} of crime. | | academic | ielts-writing |
in the long run | over a long period of time; eventually | Investing in renewables will save money {{in the long run}}. | | neutral | ielts-writing |
be a matter of debate | to be something people disagree about | Whether zoos are ethical remains {{a matter of debate}}. | | academic | ielts-writing |
from a(n) [economic] perspective | considering it from that point of view | {{From an economic perspective}}, the policy makes sense. | Swap the adjective: social, environmental, ethical. | academic | ielts-writing |
This, in turn, ... | as a result of the previous thing, another effect follows | Higher taxes reduce spending. {{This, in turn}}, slows economic growth. | Shows a chain of cause and effect, which boosts Coherence & Cohesion. | academic | ielts-writing |
To illustrate, ... | introduces an example | {{To illustrate}}, Finland's schools give little homework yet achieve excellent results. | Vary with "For instance", "A case in point is". | academic | ielts-writing |
an unprecedented [rise] | never seen before | The last decade has seen {{an unprecedented}} rise in remote work. | | academic | ielts-writing |
be at odds with | to conflict with | These findings are {{at odds with}} earlier studies. | | academic | ielts-writing |
outweigh | to be greater or more important than | The benefits of vaccination far {{outweigh}} the risks. | Classic for "Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?" questions. | academic | ielts-writing |
# ---------- IELTS Writing Task 1 (describing data) ----------
rise sharply | to increase quickly and by a lot | The number of users {{rose sharply}} between 2010 and 2015. | Also: "increased dramatically", "surged", "soared". Past: rose, risen. | academic | ielts-task1 |
peak at | to reach the highest point at | Sales {{peaked at}} 3 million units in 2018. | | academic | ielts-task1 |
level off | to stop rising or falling and stay steady | After 2015, unemployment {{levelled off}} at around 5%. | Also "plateau", "stabilise". US spelling: leveled. | academic | ielts-task1 |
fluctuate | to rise and fall irregularly | Oil prices {{fluctuated}} throughout the period. | | academic | ielts-task1 |
account for | to make up a particular proportion of | Coal {{accounted for}} 40% of electricity generation. | Very useful for pie charts. | academic | ielts-task1 |
a marginal decrease | a very small fall | There was {{a marginal decrease}} in car ownership. | Also "a slight dip". | academic | ielts-task1 |
remain stable | to stay at the same level | Spending on education {{remained stable}} over the decade. | Also "remained constant". | academic | ielts-task1 |
the overwhelming majority | nearly all | {{The overwhelming majority}} of respondents preferred online shopping. | | academic | ielts-task1 |
roughly a third | approximately 33% | {{Roughly a third}} of the budget went on healthcare. | Vary percentages with fractions: nearly half, just over a quarter. | academic | ielts-task1 |

compared with | in comparison to | Rail travel was popular in France {{compared with}} the UK. | | academic | ielts-task1 |

be glued to | to watch or use something constantly | Teenagers seem to be {{glued to}} their phones. | | informal | ielts-speaking |
